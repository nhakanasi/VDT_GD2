\section{Method}
Our system extends a pretrained zero-shot TTS model with control over non-verbal
vocalizations (NVV) in Vietnamese. Since Vietnamese speech with NVV annotations is scarce, we synthesize training data by inserting voice-converted NVV clips into real Vietnamese recordings at natural pauses, with an inline tag at the matching position in the transcript (\S\ref{sec:data}). We then fine-tune the TTS model on this data mixed . At inference, an inline tag such as \texttt{[laughter]} in the input text produces the corresponding vocalization in the cloned voice. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} gives an overview.

\subsubsection{Non-verbal vocalization corpus}
% Code: ../VocalSoundExtend (nvv/pipeline.py, nvv/nvs38k.py, nvv/fsd50k.py) and
% ../Para/scripts/build_clip_index.py.
VocalSound~\citep{vocalsound} alone is limited: it covers six classes (laughter, sigh, cough,
throat clearing, sneeze, sniff), and its crowdsourced clips often carry long silences or stray
speech from a loose cut. To add classes and keep only clean events, we pool NVV clips from
three kinds of source: (1)~other NVV corpora: the Deeply Nonverbal Vocalization dataset, whose
vocal classes we use as they are,% TODO cite
\ and FSD50K~\citep{fsd50k}, whose clip-level labels we localise in time; (2)~NVV events cut
from an existing tagged speech corpus, NonVerbalSpeech-38K (NVS-38K);% TODO cite
\ and (3)~events cut from speech generated with our transcripts by commercial TTS systems
(FishAudio, Gemini TTS).
% TODO (3): no code for this source exists in Para or VocalSoundExtend, and no such clip
% is in the clip index behind Table 1. Confirm it was used, or drop it.
For VocalSound we keep only the test and validation splits (\S\ref{sec:exp-data}).

Each source locates its events differently. For NVS-38K, whose captions carry one inline
tag, we force-align the caption words with Qwen3-ForcedAligner. The aligner tends to stretch
the neighbouring words over the non-verbal sound, so the gap between the two words around the
tag does not give the event's extent. It only confirms that the tag refers to the dataset's
own detected non-verbal region (within 1\,s), and that region is what we cut. FSD50K has
clip-level labels only, so FlexSED's frame posterior for the class prompt locates the
events (frames ${\ge}\,0.5$, gaps up to 0.3\,s merged). Clips that carry two of our target
labels are skipped, since their events could not be told apart.

Every located event is then cut and checked in the same way. Its edges are tightened to the
frame energy within $\pm$0.2\,s of the region, then padded by 75\,ms and faded by 10\,ms. The
event is kept only if (i)~FlexSED scores its class ${\ge}\,0.5$ over the sound and scores no
VocalSound class higher, so a new class does not duplicate an existing one; (ii)~no other
labelled sound of the recording scores ${\ge}\,0.5$ over it; (iii)~Qwen3-ASR finds no lexical
word in it, so no speech overlaps it; (iv)~its length is within a per-class range (e.g.
0.2--2.5\,s for breathing, 0.8--4.0\,s for a yawn); (v)~its SNR against the recording's
noise floor is at least 6\,dB; and (vi)~at most 0.1\% of its samples are clipped.

\subsubsection{Slot finding and tagging}
Following SynParaSpeech~\citep{synparaspeech}, we first find the pauses where an NVV could go and
only then let a model choose among them. Placing the tag first and aligning it afterwards
fails on connected speech: the boundary between two words is usually continuous voicing, and
on a 20-utterance pilot the nearest real pause was a median 2.02\,s away from the aligned tag.
Silero VAD finds the speech regions. Regions less than 0.10\,s apart are merged, so stop
closures and glottal breaks do not count as pauses, and the remaining gaps are trimmed by
0.05\,s at each edge and kept if at least 0.12\,s long. Silence before the first word and
after the last is ignored. ViVoice transcripts have no word timing, so Qwen3-ForcedAligner
aligns the untagged transcript to the audio. A pause becomes a \emph{slot} when it overlaps
the gap between two aligned words; if two pauses fall in one gap, the longer is kept. Each slot
records its midpoint and the character offset of the word after it. If any word cannot be
matched back to the transcript in order, the utterance gets no slots and is skipped, rather
than risk a tag between the wrong two words. The slots are marked with numbers in the
transcript, e.g. ``\viet{chiáº¿n lÆ°á»£c , <1> tá»©c lÃ  <2> nhÆ° váº­y}''.

The recording and the marked transcript go to Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct, served locally with
vLLM. The model is asked to listen to the delivery and answer in JSON with the one marker where
a non-verbal sound would most plausibly occur and the one tag from the offered list that fits
what the speaker is doing there, or \texttt{\{"slot": null\}} if no position suits any sound.
We sample with temperature 0.2 and top-$p$ 0.95. An invalid reply is retried, up to three
attempts in all, and a null answer skips the utterance. The model never writes transcript
text: the tagged transcript is built in code by inserting \texttt{[tag]} at the chosen slot's
character offset, and the splice time is the slot's midpoint, so text and audio refer to the
same point by construction.

Left alone, the model settles on a few neutral classes; on the pilot it chose only laughter
and sigh. A tag balancer therefore keeps a running count of the tags assigned so far, carried
across runs, and withholds from the offered list any tag that holds more than 25\% of them,
with the share taken over at least 20 tags. A reply that names a withheld tag is re-asked. The
tag is offered again once the other tags catch up.
% NOTE: with 14 tags a 25% cap is about 3.5x the uniform share (7%), so it only stops runaway
% tags; tagging.max_tag_share can be lowered towards 1/14 for a flatter distribution.

\subsubsection{Clip selection}
Within the chosen class, one clip is drawn at random, weighted towards clips that match the host
speaker on the properties that Seed-VC passes through unchanged. Speaker timbre, pitch level
and pitch contour are rebuilt by the conversion (with F0 conditioning off), so we do not match
on them. Clips whose peak is below 0.05 or that are shorter than 0.35\,s after trimming
silence, and clips with more than 1\% of samples at full scale, are excluded. Four measures
are taken on each trimmed clip and on the 2.5\,s of speech before the cut:
\emph{intensity}, the mean of the clip's crest-factor and duration ranks within its class,
against the rank of the context window's RMS among all windows of that length in the
utterance; \emph{clarity}, the SNR as the ratio of the 90th to the 10th percentile of 20\,ms
frame RMS (0--60\,dB, measured on the whole utterance for the speaker); \emph{tempo}, envelope
peaks per second, compared in octaves and used for laughter only, since the timing of a cough
or sneeze has nothing to do with speaking rate; and \emph{tilt}, the slope of a line fitted to
the log envelope, in dB from start to end. Each distance $d_a$ is divided by a tolerance set to
about a third of its measured spread (0.35 rank, 12\,dB, 1 octave, 18\,dB), and a clip is
drawn with probability proportional to
\begin{equation}
w = \exp\Big(-\sum_a \lambda_a d_a\Big),
\end{equation}
with weights $\lambda = 1.0, 1.5, 1.0, 0.5$ for intensity, clarity, tempo and tilt. Clarity
weighs most because VocalSound's microphones vary far more than its speakers' loudness. Tilt
has half weight because its premise, that a speaker trailing off should be followed by a
decaying event, has not been confirmed by listening. Sampling, rather than taking the closest
clip, keeps every usable clip possible, so a soft-spoken speaker still sometimes gets a loud
laugh. For the same reason the intensity target is drawn per row from
$\mathcal{N}(r, 0.2^2)$, clipped to $[0,1]$, around the speaker's own rank $r$. Clip features
are precomputed once for the whole pool, so every row samples from the full class.

\subsubsection{Voice conversion and insertion}
The selected clip is trimmed of silence, peak-normalised to 0.95 and zero-padded to at least
1\,s, as Seed-VC's input chunking requires. It is converted with Seed-VC~v1 (50 diffusion
steps, classifier-free guidance rate 0.7, length adjustment 1.0, F0 conditioning off), using
the host utterance itself as the voice reference, at 22.05\,kHz, and is trimmed again
afterwards. F0 conditioning is off because its pitch tracker (RMVPE) is built for speech and
singing and fails on coughs, sniffs and throat clearing.

The converted event's RMS is set to the RMS of the 2.5\,s of speech before the cut plus a
per-class offset (laughter $+3$\,dB, sneeze $+4$, cough $+2$, throat clearing $-2$, sigh $-4$,
sniff $-5$, and 0 for the other classes), and its peak is capped at 1.5 times the speech's
99.9th-percentile level. The recording is cut at the slot's midpoint, and a 50\,ms exponential
fade (curvature $k{=}5$) is applied to all four edges: the end of the speech before the cut,
both ends of the event and the start of the speech after it. An exponential fade reaches half
level within the first 14\% of its length, which keeps the sharp onset of a laugh or cough
that a cosine fade would soften. Each side of the event should then carry about as much silence
as the speaker normally pauses for. The target pauses are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T_{\mathrm{pre}} &= \bar{T}_{p}\cdot s_g\cdot s_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(c)}\\
T_{\mathrm{post}} &= \bar{T}_{p}\cdot s_g\cdot s_{\mathrm{post}}^{(c)}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{T}_{p}$ is the median gap between voiced stretches of the utterance, clamped to
$[0.04, 0.40]$\,s, $s_g{=}1$ is a global scale, and $(s_{\mathrm{pre}}^{(c)},
s_{\mathrm{post}}^{(c)})$ are per-class scales. Events that end on emptied lungs need a longer
beat after them: laughter $(0.6, 1.4)$, sigh $(0.5, 1.2)$, cough and throat clearing
$(0.7, 1.0)$, sneeze $(0.8, 1.3)$, sniff $(0.5, 0.7)$, and $(1, 1)$ for the other classes.
% NOTE: the earlier draft said these scales were measured from the pauses around each tag. In
% the code (Para/para_synth/vocalsound.py, GAP_SHAPE) they are priors set from this
% respiratory reasoning, not measurements, and only the six VocalSound classes have them.
% Measure them, or keep this wording.
The target is a total, not an addition: the inserted silence is
$\max(0, T - T_{\mathrm{exist}})$, where $T_{\mathrm{exist}}$ is the silence already on that
side of the cut, so the speaker's own pause is never shortened. Inserted silence is filled
with the recording's own background noise, its quietest 100\,ms tiled with crossfaded seams,
since generated white noise audibly hissed. Finally the whole recording is peak-normalised to
0.95 and written as 16-bit PCM at 22.05\,kHz.

\subsubsection{Quality filtering}
Every finished row is scored with NISQA~v2.0~\citep{nisqa}, a no-reference predictor of the
mean opinion score (MOS) and of four quality dimensions, on both the source recording and the
spliced one. A row is kept if (1)~its NISQA MOS drops by ${\le}\,0.5$ from source to result,
(2)~its NISQA discontinuity drops by ${\le}\,0.22$, catching an audible seam at the splice, and
(3)~its boundary activity at the cut is ${\le}\,1.2$. The thresholds apply to the drop rather
than to absolute scores, because an absolute floor mostly measures how clean the input corpus
was. Boundary activity covers what NISQA cannot hear once the fades and the silence have
smoothed the junctions. It is measured on the source recording as the RMS of the 50\,ms on
each side of the cut divided by the RMS of that whole side (0 means the cut is in silence, about
1 means it is in speech of average loudness), and a row is judged by its worse side. The
thresholds were set on the 20-utterance pilot: the MOS threshold removes one clear outlier, and
the other two were chosen by listening. Seed-VC's diffusion is not seeded, so a rejection means
that one conversion was bad, and re-rendering a rejected row may produce a passing take.

\subsection{Model adaptation}\label{sec:adapt}
We build on OmniVoice~\citep{omnivoice}, a massively multilingual zero-shot TTS model that maps
text directly to multi-codebook acoustic tokens with a non-autoregressive diffusion language
model and clones a voice from a reference recording. The released model cannot generate
Vietnamese NVVs as it stands, for three reasons. First, its tag set is small: of its 13 inline
tags, only \texttt{[laughter]} and \texttt{[sigh]} are non-verbal sounds, the other 11 being
lexical interjections such as \texttt{[question-ah]} and \texttt{[surprise-wa]}, so 12 of our
14 classes (Table~\ref{tab:nvv}) have no tag at all. Second, it has never seen a tag in
Vietnamese. About 8.5k hours of Vietnamese speech, ViVoice among it, are in its training data,
but all of it is untagged; its tagged data come from Chinese and English corpora (NVSpeech,
NVS-38K and ParaSpeechCaps).% TODO cite NVSpeech, ParaSpeechCaps
\ Third, it was not trained for tag control: these corpora are a small fraction of its 581k
hours of training data, and the OmniVoice paper does not evaluate tag control. In practice the
released model often drops the tag. Given Vietnamese text, it produces no vocalization at the
tag for XX\% of \texttt{[laughter]} and \texttt{[sigh]} tags, the two it does support, and for
XX\% of the other 12 (NVMR, \S\ref{sec:eval} and Table~\ref{tab:main}).

We therefore fine-tune OmniVoice on the synthetic data of \S\ref{sec:data}, which supplies all
14 tags on Vietnamese speech, mixed with untagged ViVoice utterances to preserve ordinary
Vietnamese synthesis (\S\ref{sec:exp-data}). Nothing else changes: the training objective is
OmniVoice's own, and at inference the tag is written inline in the input text, as with
OmniVoice's tags.

\subsection{Evaluation}\label{sec:eval}
\textbf{Test set.} The test set has 420 real Vietnamese sentences, 30 per class, each with one
tag. Each sentence is chosen so that the tag fits its content, e.g.\ ``\viet{bật cười}'' (burst
out laughing) for laughter, ``\viet{hoảng hốt}'' (startled) for gasp, ``\viet{ngủ say}'' (sound
asleep) for snoring, and ``\viet{hạt tiêu}'' (pepper) or ``\viet{trời rét}'' (bitter cold) for
sneeze. The tag sits where the sound would naturally occur, after the action word or at a
comma. About 65\% of tags are mid-sentence; throat clearing and breathing lean towards the start
of the sentence, and snoring and sniffing towards the end. A few sentences were lightly edited
to fix spelling or cut a garbled ending. Sentences are 8--30 words long (median 16),
with no digits and almost no English words, so the WER below is not inflated by the ASR
writing out numbers or mishearing foreign words. Each sentence is synthesised once for each of
XX held-out speakers, prompted with a XX\,s reference recording and its transcript, giving XX
items per system.

\textbf{Synthesis.} Every system uses OmniVoice's voice-cloning inference with the language set
to Vietnamese, and the same item gets the same random seed in every system. The released
inference code tokenises an inline tag apart from the text around it, whereas training
tokenises the whole string, so a model can miss or garble tags it has learned. We therefore
tokenise the whole string at inference for every system, the released model included, so that
all systems receive the same input.

\textbf{Objective metrics.} NVPA, WER and SS follow Track~A of the VLSP~2026 NVV TTS
task.% TODO cite
\ Each metric is reported on its own, as the mean over all items of a system unless stated
otherwise; no combined score is computed. Higher is better for every metric except NVMR and WER.
% TODO cite FlexSED, ECAPA-TDNN, Zipformer.

\emph{NV placement accuracy (NVPA)} checks whether a vocalization of the tagged class occurs
where the tag sits in the text. The tag is removed and the remaining words are force-aligned to
the 16\,kHz output with a Vietnamese wav2vec2 CTC model
(\nolinkurl{nguyenvulebinh/wav2vec2-base-vietnamese-250h}) and torchaudio's
\texttt{forced\_align}. Each word boundary is a slot, and the tag sits at one of them.
Qwen3-ForcedAligner is not used here: besides stretching words over non-verbal sounds
(\S\ref{sec:data}), on Vietnamese it collapses runs of words to one instant. FlexSED then scores
each class prompt over the whole recording, in 10\,s windows with a 5\,s hop, and each frame
keeps its highest posterior across windows. A class's events are runs of frames above 0.2 that
reach the class threshold at least once. The threshold is 0.5 for most classes, 0.4 for throat
clearing, and 0.7 for breathing, which FlexSED also scores at 0.5--0.7 on pauses where no
breath is heard. Runs less than 0.12\,s apart are merged, events shorter than 0.08\,s are
dropped, and events shorter than 0.25\,s that touch either end of the clip are ignored as
cut-off tails. A tag is \emph{correct} if an event of its class lies within 3\,s of its slot
with no aligned word in between; a word that overlaps the event by at most 0.05\,s does not
count as in between. Otherwise the tag is \emph{misplaced} if exactly one word separates the
nearest event of its class from the slot, \emph{wrong class} if only another class fires at the
slot, and \emph{missed} if no event of any class is at the slot. Classes are scored strictly: a
sigh, pant or gasp also triggers FlexSED's breathing prompt, but counts as correct only when its
own class fires. NVPA is the share of correct tags (0--1).
% NOTE (../Para/eval/METRICS.md): the thresholds for laughter, throat clearing, sniff and
% breathing were set by listening; the 0.5 of the other ten classes is a placeholder. Calibrate
% them before reporting per-tag NVPA for those classes.

\emph{NV miss rate (NVMR)} is the share of missed tags (0--1): the model made no sound at the
tag, either skipping it or reading it out as words. NVPA counts a missing, a misplaced and a
wrong-class event alike as failures; NVMR isolates the first.

\emph{Word error rate (WER)} is computed with a Vietnamese Zipformer
(\nolinkurl{sherpa-onnx-zipformer-vi-2025-04-20}) using greedy search on the 16\,kHz output.
The reference and the transcript are normalised alike: tags are removed, the text is put in
Unicode NFC and lower-cased, and every character other than a letter or digit becomes a space.
WER is computed at corpus level over all items of a system, not as a mean of per-item WERs,
and reported in percent.

\emph{Speaker similarity (SS)} is the cosine between the ECAPA-TDNN embeddings
(\nolinkurl{speechbrain/spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb}) of the output and of the speaker's reference
recording, both at 16\,kHz and L2-normalised (0--1 in practice).

\textbf{Subjective metric.} The only subjective metric is MOS. XX native Vietnamese listeners
each hear one sample at a time and rate its overall quality on a five-point scale (1 = bad,
5 = excellent). Each system contributes XX samples, and we report the mean MOS with a 95\%
confidence interval.
